PART 1: STRATEGIC CONTEXT (Read this before touching any copy)
1.1 What the site is today
The current site positions Dunmore Bay Advisors as a full-spectrum, sector-agnostic M&A execution advisor serving founders selling companies, PE sponsors doing add-ons, and corporate development teams doing carve-outs. It covers buy-side and sell-side, cross-border deals, JVs, distressed M&A, and post-merger integration. It speaks the language of institutional M&A (SPAs, carve-outs, bulge-bracket comparison, $2B+ transaction value).
1.2 What the site must become
A site that speaks to exactly ONE person: the ETA searcher — a self-funded or lightly-backed individual determined to buy and run one small business, typically financed with an SBA 7(a) loan, who needs hands-on execution support to actually get a deal closed.
1.3 The target archetype (write every sentence for this person)
* Individual searcher (self-funded, traditional search fund, or searching-while-employed)
* Often a former banker, consultant, PE professional, military officer, or corporate operator
* Buying a business typically in the $500K–$5M SDE / EBITDA range


CONFIRMED DECISION — state the deal-size range explicitly on the site. The site must name the target range "$500K–$5M SDE" (Seller's Discretionary Earnings) in visible, load-bearing copy — not buried in a list. This is a deliberate qualification filter: it improves lead quality at the cost of some top-of-funnel breadth, and the client has approved that trade-off. Use "$500K–$5M SDE" as the canonical formatting (see Section B, C, F, and H for required placements).


* Financing with SBA 7(a) debt, often with a seller note and sometimes small equity investors
* Has read the HBR Guide, listened to the podcasts, maybe joined an accelerator — but is discovering that content is not the same as execution support
* Pain points (these drive ALL the copy):
   1. Doesn't know where to start or how to sequence the journey
   2. Spinning wheels reviewing deals that go nowhere
   3. Gets to LOI and then stalls — overwhelmed by driving a process to close
   4. Terrified of missing the big diligence questions and buying a lemon
   5. Burned out on accelerators/communities that give shallow advice but no real in-the-trenches support
   6. Searching part-time while employed and needs someone to keep the process moving
   7. Intimidated by SBA lender requirements, sources & uses, and deal structuring
   8. Negotiating alone against an experienced seller and their broker
1.4 The core promise (the through-line of the whole site)
"You don't need another course. You need someone in the deal with you." Dunmore Bay is the searcher's deal-side operator: a former investment banking and private equity professional who has personally executed dozens of transactions and now sits shoulder-to-shoulder with searchers from first CIM to closing table, purpose-built for SBA-funded acquisitions.
1.5 Voice and tone rules
* Direct, empathetic, operator-to-operator. Second person ("you") throughout.
* Name the searcher's fears explicitly. Specificity = credibility (say "LOI", "Q of E", "SBA 7(a)", "sources & uses", "seller note", "DSCR" — this audience knows the vocabulary and it signals we live in their world).
* Kill all institutional M&A language: delete "carve-outs," "bulge-bracket," "corporate development," "cross-border," "joint ventures," "distressed M&A," "PE sponsor," "SPA" (use "purchase agreement" instead), "platform add-on."
* Confident but honest. No fake urgency, no guru energy. This audience is allergic to hype after being oversold by accelerators.
* Keep sentences short. This is a "finally, someone gets it" site, not a whitepaper.
1.6 What to REMOVE entirely from the site
* All sell-side positioning (divestitures, "founder selling your life's work")
* Carve-outs, spin-offs, joint ventures, cross-border, distressed M&A
* PE sponsor and corporate development audience references
* Post-Merger Integration as a headline service (fold a light version into "after closing" support)
* The "Sectors" list (Technology & SaaS, Financial Services etc.) — replace per Part 2, Section H
* "$2B+ Transaction Value Advised" stat in its current framing (see revised stats — big institutional numbers can actually alienate this audience if not reframed as "I bring big-deal rigor to your small deal")

Stop wasting months on deals that were never going to work. We help you define your acquisition criteria, build your sourcing approach, rapidly screen CIMs and broker listings, and pressure-test seller financials before you fall in love — so your energy goes only into deals worth chasing.


Card 2 — Valuation, Offers & LOI


Know what the business is actually worth and what a lender will finance. We build the model — SDE and EBITDA normalization, debt service coverage, sources & uses — help you set your offer, and draft an LOI with terms that protect you without scaring off the seller.


Card 3 — SBA Financing Support


The SBA 7(a) process makes or breaks most searcher deals. We prepare your lender package, build the sources & uses, structure the seller note and any equity injection to satisfy SBA rules, help you compare lender term sheets, and manage the bank's checklist so financing never becomes the bottleneck.


Card 4 — Due Diligence Management


Sleep at night knowing the big questions got asked. We build and run your diligence plan — financial, operational, legal, customer, and people — coordinate your Quality of Earnings provider and attorney, chase down every open item, and translate findings into a clear picture: proceed, renegotiate, or walk.


Card 5 — Negotiation & Purchase Agreement Advisory


Don't negotiate alone against a seller and broker who've done this before. We're at your side through price adjustments, working capital targets, reps and warranties, indemnities, seller transition terms, and non-competes — bridging what your attorney drafts and what the deal actually needs.


Card 6 — Process Management to Close (and Day 1)


The distance from LOI to closing is where searchers get overwhelmed. We run the master checklist, coordinate lender, attorney, accountant, landlord, and insurer, hold everyone to the timeline, and drive the deal to the closing table — then help you plan a clean Day 1 and first 90 days as the new owner.
